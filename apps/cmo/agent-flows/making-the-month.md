# Making the month

**When:** right after the owner approves the strategy and pays, and again for each new month.

**Cuso does**

0. From cmo-v22, Core first plans the month the bought plan makes, right after payment (and again after an upgrade, adding the bigger plan's posts). Core decides every post's day, network, format, topic (pillar), kind (mix) and funnel step itself, so each topic and kind holds its share on every network, and the month holds at least the plan's minimum ("At least N posts a month" on Subscribe). One short video in four is a talking video (17, 28 and 49 a month on Starter, Growth and Scale with all six networks). Each one also goes out on Facebook as a Reel and takes the place of one of Facebook's own posts that week: a link post or its weekly Reel first, a picture post last, so Facebook keeps its pace (owner, 2026-11-25). With all six networks that leaves Facebook 3, 0 and 7 picture posts of its own on Starter, Growth and Scale, down from 12, 18 and 39. One a week also goes out on LinkedIn, which stays slow and carries its other posts. All of them go out on TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts. A month without short videos turns one lead-network post in three into a talking video (X and LinkedIn: 6, 9 and 9). Core never plans more talking videos than the plan's credits pay for: at most 70% of them across the month's pieces, with each talking video counted at 251 credits, its longest script with the site, B-roll and music. The other 30% is kept for Cuso's own work and remakes. Then `openai/gpt-6.2-sol` writes each post's title, brief and visual, one network-week per call, eight calls at once, every call told all the topics and their shares. Core checks the merged month for posts repeating another's title or point across calls and has those rewritten (two rounds at most). The strategy's 3 to 5 example posts stay as they are. The owner sees "Cuso is planning your month"; a failed write is retried (taken over after 15 minutes, every 3 hours after three tries, with an alert), and an unfinished write keeps what it wrote. A made month below its minimum raises `cmo_month_below_floor` once. Strategies written before cmo-v22 keep their whole calendar and skip this step.
1. Writes the pieces of the calendar the bought plan makes (Starter, Growth or Scale; all of it on Tester credits), in the strategy's mix and pillars, in the audience's language. From cmo-v8 each calendar entry carries the one claim it argues: the strategy review names a claim argued twice in a week or more than three times in the month, and once the month is in Core gives the later pieces of a repeated claim their own, and moves every piece past the third on one point (across all networks) onto a point of its own: a new title, brief and claim from the Brand Brain's facts, before the owner approves. A story is asked only of a social-proof piece. From cmo-v20 Core first gives every piece still without a claim its point, in a call of its own before any words are written: it lists the Brand Brain's facts once each and holds one fact to about one piece in sixteen on a network (X at 99 posts: 7), so no single point leads the month. It also marks the offer pieces the funnel promises, two a week on each network of its action stage: each ends with the offer's action, with its link in the first comment on X, LinkedIn and Facebook or "link in bio" on Instagram and TikTok, and Core sends one back without it. Each chunk of the writer reads only its own network's writing skill. From cmo-v21 the industry research's items are facts beside the Brand Brain's: most helpful pieces rest on one, and a claim on one ends with its source; the writer takes industry facts only from that research and puts a news source's link in the first comment on X, LinkedIn and Facebook.
2. Picks an old print from the reference library for every picture, a different one per piece, and the few words it carries; a carousel gets one print for all its slides and words per slide. From cmo-v8 the prints vary in kind: an infographic only for a picture that shows numbers or a comparison, at most one picture in four, and neighbouring pieces on one network never in the same kind (Core picks within these, and refuses a save that breaks them). Where a picture must show the real site or product, he names that brand picture (a page screenshot, a section of a page, a photo).
3. Core makes each picture in that print's look with the brand's colours and logo, places the named brand picture itself (unchanged, in a browser frame on a panel the picture leaves free; the model never redraws it). A named page shows the way the format needs it: on a phone in a short, reel or story, on a computer or as one of its sections in a feed picture, another section per piece so a month does not repeat one shot. From cmo-v20 he finds any page of the site, its facts and its screenshots in sitePages and the page's note. Core checks each picture (words spelled right, nothing copied from the print, nothing invented on the brand's page). A picture that fails is made again, on a stronger model from the third try; one that fails every try comes back to him for a new visual and print. When he changes a piece's words or visual after its picture is on the post, Core takes the old picture off, makes it again and schedules the post at its time once the new one is on. Videos are made from the brand's own pictures; a piece that names none starts at another of them.
4. Schedules each post on its connected network. A piece for a network that isn't connected is made all the same (words, picture or video) and waits finished; it is scheduled once the owner connects the account. From cmo-v5 on the month holds every network CMO offers, so most owners have such pieces.
5. Jev reads the month against the writing skills and the brand voice; one rewrite run fixes what misses, including a piece whose words argue another piece's claim. The review records how many pictures took each kind of print.

**The owner should see:** one progress card in chat, then a full Calendar where every made piece opens; nothing goes out that isn't on it.

**Missing today:** a way for the owner to turn the old-print look off, or to pick the prints.
